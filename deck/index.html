Why now03 / 19

Global opportunities are easier to find. Knowing which ones are right for you is another challenge.

Remote job listings can open doors to international opportunities, but a “Remote” label alone doesn’t tell you whether you can work from Indonesia, whether the hours fit your lifestyle, or what the salary means in rupiah.

Finding an opportunity should come with clarity, not guesswork.

UpNext brings essential job details together, helping you understand what is known, what remains uncertain, and what to consider before applying.

Four information gaps04 / 19
01

Location eligibility.

A remote label doesn’t always clarify which countries an employer can hire from or what work authorization is required.

02

Working-hour compatibility.

Time-zone expectations can be difficult to assess without translating the schedule into your local time.

03

Salary context.

Foreign-currency salaries need local context to understand their estimated value in rupiah.

04

Company transparency.

Job listings rarely tell the whole story. Understanding a company also means looking beyond its own description and exploring available employee perspectives.

Solution05 / 19

UpNext reads remote listings from public sources, then answers three questions before you apply:

Whether you can really apply from Indonesia.

How the hours fall in WIB.

What the salary is worth in rupiah.

Key assumptions06 / 19
01

UpNext doesn’t take postings from companies; it reads listings that belong to someone else.

So eligibility data is never guaranteed, and the level of confidence always has to be shown.

02

Users will share their working-hour limits and minimum salary if they’re told what it’s for.

03

The decision to apply is made before applying, and that’s where the leverage is.

Deliberately not built07 / 19

Moved to the roadmap, still visible in the menu as a note.

Only the visible traces are reported.

Left out on purpose.

AI Interaction Breakdown14 / 19

Role

NEX reads and translates; the user decides.

NEX never sends an application, never contacts an employer, and never decides which roles to pursue.

Input from the user
location and time zone working-hour limits minimum salary target role experience priorities
Input from the listing
eligibility text overlap hours salary range listing source where applications go the poster’s wording
Output

One verdict per dimension, always with a quote from the listing.

One statement about who is hiring. Never a verdict on authenticity.

Uncertainty

Three explicit states:

confirmed unconfirmed not mentioned

Unverified roles never rank first.

Roles you can’t apply to get a verdict, not a score.

User control

Every input can be changed in Review profile, and verdicts are recalculated.

Every verdict traces back to one quoted line.

When data is missing, NEX offers a next step, not a guess.

Deliberate limits15 / 19

Not AI, just reading a URL: where the apply button goes.

careers.karyacloud.commeans the company itself.
jobs.lever.co/agencynamemeans it isn’t.
linkedin.com/jobsmeans the application never leaves LinkedIn.

The real AI work: reading the listing’s prose.

  • Work-permit requirements buried in a paragraph.
  • Overlap hours written in another time zone.
  • Agency language like “our client, a leading fintech company”.

Out of reach, and we don’t claim it:

  • confirming the company is honest,
  • or that the role will really be filled.

“UpNext doesn’t verify companies. NEX reports the traces it can see, and when they aren’t enough, it says they aren’t enough.”

Design rationale 3 of 318 / 19
